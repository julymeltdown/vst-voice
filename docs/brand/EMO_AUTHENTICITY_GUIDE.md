# Emo Authenticity Guide

This guide is a design constraint, not a gatekeeping test for real people or fans.

## Core interpretation

The character should reflect tension between restraint and emotional rupture, ordinary fan clothing, and coherent early/mid-2000s emo visual language. Authenticity is not demonstrated through obscure trivia, fake scene history, or accumulated alternative-fashion symbols.

## Allowed signals

Use a limited subset:

- asymmetric dark layered hair;
- restrained eyeliner;
- worn hoodie/cardigan/T-shirt layering;
- slim dark jeans;
- worn canvas shoes;
- one or two functional repairs or splice-inspired seams;
- a single restrained accent color.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- `EMO`, `SAD`, `BROKEN`, `DIY`, or similar identity declarations;
- fake band logos, tickets, backstage passes, tour histories, or zines;
- self-harm, blood, razor, medical-bandage, or permanent-tear imagery;
- microphone/stage/frontperson pose as the canonical identity;
- automatic mixing of goth, scene, visual-kei, cyberpunk, and metalcore clichés;
- decorative chains, fishnet, crosses, bats, and safety pins added without function;
- sexualized generic “alternative girl” silhouette as the primary product hook.

## Review test

Hide all descriptive text and ask reviewers:

1. What music/culture and time period does this suggest?
2. Does it look like everyday clothing or a stage costume?
3. Does any element feel calculated or fake?
4. Is goth, scene, visual kei, or techwear stronger than emo?
5. Does the figure appear to be a vocalist despite the product definition?
